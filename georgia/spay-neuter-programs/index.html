Georgia Low-Cost Spay/Neuter Programs and Southwest Georgia Clinics

Last checked: November 18, 2026. Prices and hours change — call before you go.

Georgia has no single state-run program that pays for your pet's surgery. The state's Dog and Cat Sterilization Program funds shelters and rescues, not individuals, so most people use a nonprofit clinic or a SpayGeorgia certificate. Below are the programs, then Southwest Georgia clinics.

Statewide programs

SpayGeorgia low-cost certificates
Cover routine spay/neuter surgery (prep, anesthesia, procedure, routine hospitalization, stitch removal). Not covered: vaccinations, disease testing, deworming, other procedures. Not free. Certificates cannot be issued online: request the information packet (price list and participating vets), then mail the order form with payment. Certificates are void six months after issue and cannot be combined with other discounts. Phone (800) 706-7081. Source.
Georgia Dept. of Agriculture — Dog and Cat Sterilization Program (DCSP)
Funded by the sale of three dog and cat license plates. The grant “does not give money to individuals”; licensed rescues, local government shelters and veterinary medical foundations can apply, and individuals can then ask those grant recipients for help. Source.

A directory of spay/neuter partners by county served: fixgeorgiapets.org.
